How much protein do you actually need?

By Dana Whitlock, 14 June 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer for most people lifting three to five times a week is 1.6 to 2.2 grams per kilogram of bodyweight per day. Below that range you leave muscle on the table in a deficit. Above it the returns flatten out fast, and the extra calories are usually better spent on carbohydrate around training.

Why the range is a range

Leaner lifters in a steep deficit sit at the top of it, because protein is doing double duty: supporting recovery and blunting muscle loss. Someone in a modest surplus with plenty of training experience can sit near the bottom and be fine. Bodyweight is the practical anchor; if you carry a lot of fat mass, scale to lean mass or to goal weight instead.

Distribution matters less than the total

The old advice about 30 g every three hours has not held up well. Total daily intake is the lever. That said, three or four feedings of 0.4 g/kg each is an easy way to hit the total without thinking about it, and it tends to help with fullness.

Sources worth the effort

Whole foods first: eggs, dairy, poultry, fish, lean beef, tofu, tempeh, legumes. Powder is a convenience, not a category. If you are plant-based, push the total nearer 2.0 g/kg and lean on soy, pea, and mixed sources so the amino acid profile takes care of itself.

What to do on Monday

Weigh yourself, multiply by 1.8, and treat that number in grams as the floor for a fortnight. Track honestly for those two weeks, then stop tracking and keep the habits that survived.
